# Overarching story points

Implementation contract: main-story events use explicit progression triggers through the [shared event system](event_system.md). Side opportunities may use chance; either activation path can start the same environmental handler. Triggering an event does not automatically solve its puzzle or grant its outcome. Story content below remains proposed until reviewed with the user.


## Narrative contract

Create a child-centered emotional journey expressed through repeated objects, actions, changing relationships, and discoveries. Ichigo has no specified gender or exact age. The oversized jersey belongs to their dad and carries 15; its deeper meaning and the dad's circumstances remain undecided. Do not infer death, abandonment, or a missing-parent plot from those details.

The player advances the story through puzzles and meaningful interactions. No cutscenes, required prose journals, long dialogue, or correct global travel direction. Chance changes which situations reveal an essential idea; authored knowledge and consequence relationships hold the story together.

## Proposed emotional spine

The following is a working structure to flesh out together, not finalized plot or novel canon.

| Story point | Emotional change | Required play | Variable delivery |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0: Something familiar | The child has a small point of connection in an immense place | Handle/notice a recurring personal object or gesture while learning movement | Starting composition can vary gently; baseline equipment remains reliable |
| S1: I can affect this world | Curiosity becomes confidence | Solve a small physical problem with an observable consequence | Different eligible salvage or line-routing encounters |
| S2: I can understand it | Recognize relationships across separate encounters | Apply a previously observed pattern with a tool | Fish, habitat, or condition-based evidence paths |
| S3: What I do matters | Earlier choices acquire personal significance | Revisit a motif through an altered situation and make a consequential action | Story tracks relevant actions; local situations differ |
| S4: A connection becomes possible | The journey reaches a satisfying change | Perform a final puzzle/action built from learned verbs | An eligible concluding encounter offers the opportunity in any travel direction |

The ending could concern a connection, reunion, or another form of resolution. Choose its specific meaning in the story workshop before producing final assets. A visible destination can be a local concluding encounter without becoming a hidden fixed-coordinate objective, provided its appearance and approach are coherent.

## Knowledge graph and chance

Model milestones as predicates over knowledge/capabilities/consequences, not kilometres travelled or compass bearing. For example, S2 may require observing a pattern and successfully applying the appropriate interaction; simply owning an item does not prove the player understands it.

Each required knowledge predicate has at least two viable evidence sources in the content plan. A milestone can make several new encounter types eligible, after which the director chooses among valid opportunities. Anti-stall rules preserve access to a meaningful opportunity after a bounded run of misses; completion still depends on player action.

Story progress, evidence, unique rewards, and consequential choices persist in saves. A rolled encounter is not rerolled on camera movement or load. Repeated evidence may reinforce a motif but must not award the same milestone twice. Failed encounters can alter later presentation without removing every path forward.

Keep major reveals legible in different orders. Do not refer to an object the player has never encountered as if it were familiar. Gate a reference on its observation state or supply an alternative presentation.

## Minimal-text storytelling tools

Use a small motif vocabulary: jersey silhouette/number, a recognizable gesture, a physical keepsake if selected, a repeated shape/material, or a short sound phrase with a visual counterpart. Their meaning develops through changed context. The jersey number is part of the character's design, not an arbitrary numeric combination-lock answer.

Important interactions keep the world and the player's controls present. Closer camera framing can emphasize hands or an object within validated asset coverage. Allow the player to look, act, hesitate, or withdraw; do not disguise a cutscene as a long locked animation.

Knowledge records can be sketches and connections. Avoid replacing minimal text with an unreadable symbolic code. Test whether players infer the emotional change and practical next possibility without being coached.

## First slice and tasks

Implement S1 as one complete story beat, preceded by a light S0 motif. End the slice with a playable consequence or new possibility, not a title card explaining what the player accomplished. Final-story S2–S4 remain planning until that beat works.

1. Flesh out three short premise options together and select the relationship, motivation, and ending action.
2. Choose a small motif set that the camera and chosen art style can show clearly.
3. Specify milestone predicates, evidence alternatives, and consequence references in a graph.
4. Map S1 to the prototype puzzle and validate two chance-delivered access routes.
5. Play several event orders, including missed/failed encounters, and revise unclear references.

Acceptance: the milestone requires meaningful play, works in more than one event sequence, survives a missed clue, and has no global heading requirement. The player's understanding should come from the experience; internal story-state labels never need to appear on screen.
